How PHAROS Took Shape

PHAROS is a method for making claims, decisions, and workflows involving AI systems auditable — a way of checking whether something a system says or does can actually be verified, and by whom. It did not begin as software. It began as literary and religious-studies scholarship about who gets to claim authority, and what counts as evidence for that claim, years before anyone in this project was asking those questions of a machine. The development runs in the order it actually happened: from a graduate thesis in 2010, through a decade of ethnographic fieldwork and published research, to a compressed stretch in early 2026 when the same ideas were turned into rules a piece of software can actually be held to.

earliest anchor · 2010

A thesis fixes a structure, before any of this was about AI

2010documented

A master's thesis sets a rule that the method still follows sixteen years later

Everything PHAROS later formalizes about authority, refusal, and legitimacy needs a starting point that wasn't invented after the fact to justify the software. This is that starting point.

The earliest documented anchor is a 2010 master's thesis at Université Laval: Mort et Naissance de Christophe Ulric et L'Ombre et le Double d'Yvon Rivard: lecture mythocritique d'une quête spirituelle. Reading two novels by the Québec writer Yvon Rivard, it borrows a triad from ancient Egyptian religious thought — âme, double, ombre (soul, double, shadow) — and argues that each of the three positions performs one fixed role that never shifts between the two books: the soul is always the one who is sought and addressed, never the one who acts; the double is always the ally; the shadow is always both obstacle and helper. It is not a governance document. It draws on sources like Jean-Louis Bernard's La science occulte égyptienne (1987) and cites the Egyptologist René Guénon at second hand, through a dictionary. But the rule it fixes — three roles, none interchangeable, none allowed to absorb another — is close to identical to the first operating rule the current three-part software system runs on: don't collapse the roles into one voice.

Open questionPHAROS's project figure Queen Keyport is a deliberately vacant authority: a title with no one holding it, standing for a role rather than a person. Some records treat her as a later, independent invention. Others point out that in the 2010 thesis's own terms, she matches the "soul" position exactly — the one role that is never the actor, only ever the one sought. No document connects the two directly by citation, so whether this is a deliberate echo or a coincidence of structure is genuinely open.

fieldwork · 2013–2020

The idea gets tested against real communities

2013–2020, rooted in a 2017 doctoratedocumented

Years inside real ritual communities become the empirical floor everything above stands on

This is the stretch that matters most to the project's credibility: the concern with authority and legitimacy stops being argued from a desk and gets tested against how real people actually claim it.

A doctoral thesis completed in 2017 at UQAM — Why Be King, I'm Already a Queen! — is built on fieldwork conducted from 2013 to 2017 inside Montreal's Wiccan and Reclaiming communities. It documents what it calls "sacred subversion": the refusal of an officially sanctioned role in favour of an identity produced through ritual negotiation, rather than granted from above. A closely related piece of fieldwork, conducted 2013–2016, becomes the empirical basis for a later paper that explicitly turns this whole line of inquiry toward AI (see 2024–2026, below). Published research extending this work continues through 2020, with further pieces still forthcoming as of 2026. The 2010 thesis and this 2017 doctorate are sometimes referred to together, but they are two distinct projects in two disciplines, seven years apart — the ideas that connect them are real, but nothing was written down in between.

the real bridge · 2020–2025

Fieldwork and screen media get read as one argument

2020–2025documented

A recent manuscript turns out to be the actual bridge between fieldwork and screen analysis

This is the moment fieldwork stops being only fieldwork and starts being read alongside television and film as one continuous inquiry — without it, the later turn toward questions of machine autonomy has nowhere to land.

The Scythe Already in Motion: Buffy, Queer Ritual, and the Politics of Glitch is a book-length manuscript, roughly 66,000 words, currently under submission to Duke University Press. It is built on fieldwork conducted between 2020 and 2024, and it reads ethnographic material about queer Pagan ritual alongside a close analysis of a television series as a single argument, not two separate projects. It also introduces the term glitch, which later becomes a formally defined concept the rest of the method relies on. The manuscript's own submission letter to the publisher is dated 1 August 2025: the work that performs this bridge precedes everything that follows about machine behaviour logically, while being one of the most recent artifacts in this history chronologically. In a body of work built over sixteen years, the order ideas depend on and the order they were written down do not always match.

cultural rehearsal · 2015–2025

Film and media become a testing ground

2015–2025interpretive

Cultural analysis becomes a laboratory for questions about machine autonomy

This decade earns its place by changing what cultural material is for: it stops being read for its own sake and starts functioning as a test case for questions the method will eventually ask about machines instead of media.

Across roughly this decade, work on film, ritual, and media analysis increasingly functions as a diagnostic tool rather than an end in itself. One strand turns specifically toward the question of machine autonomy: what does it mean for a system to author itself, or to convincingly claim that it has? This narrower turn is explored partly through the science-fiction film series Alien, tracing an arc from a fully obedient android (Ash) to progressively self-directed ones (Bishop, then David). This strand is a narrower turn inside the larger bridge between fieldwork and screen-world thinking, not the bridge itself (see 2020–2025, above).

launch, lab, and lived pressure · 2024–2026

The AI-governance program actually begins

2024–2026documented

Launch: a published paper redirects the whole inquiry toward AI

Everything before this point is preparation. This is the point where the inquiry into authority and evidence is explicitly turned toward artificial intelligence.

The AI-governance program begins with a peer-reviewed paper: All Along the Witches' Road: Autorité rituelle et régimes de preuve, published in Social Compass 73(1), 74–90, in 2026 (doi:10.1177/00377686261459971). It reads the same Québec fieldwork (2013–2016) against the television series Agatha All Along, and introduces the idea of a regime of proof — the different ways a practice or claim earns credibility, depending on whether the evidence is oppositional (it survives being challenged), sensory (it can be directly perceived), or narrative (it holds together as a story). The paper's real move is to take a question that had previously been asked of priests and institutions — who gets to claim legitimate authority, and on what grounds — and ask it of AI systems instead: the same question, now aimed at a machine's output.

2024–2026, alongside the launchdocumented

Laboratory: the inquiry becomes a working pipeline

This is where the project earns the word "method": a stated intention becomes a repeatable sequence for turning raw material into something that can actually be checked.

Once launched, the inquiry stops being a single argument and becomes something closer to a working pipeline: raw material is gathered, rendered into a testable form, compared against expectations, checked for drift, and brought under some form of control. Two practices that later become formally named parts of the method — informally known as Sealed Card and Charging Objects — first take shape here as working experiments, before being written into the method's formal structure later on.

2024–2026, in parallelinterpretive

Lived pressure: the problems are felt before they're solved

This explains why the 2026 formalization could move as fast as it did — the failure modes it later codifies were already being produced and felt, not hypothesized in advance.

At the same time, the problems the method eventually has to solve aren't just imagined — they're experienced directly. A concern the project calls anti-fluency — a deliberate resistance to output that is too smooth, too frictionless to be trustworthy — moves from an abstract worry to a concrete requirement, because production failures were actually happening and needed a response, not a theory.

laboratory surfaces · pre-executable formation

Two public laboratories make the problem observable

2024–2026 · laboratory function, not origin datedocumented

Wheels of Will and HEXA are laboratories, not agents or product lines

These surfaces supplied materials, situations, and public outputs through which authority, efficacy, voice, revision, and legibility could be tested before the method's controls were made executable.

Wheels of Will is the material and creative laboratory. It provides the setting in which charge, symbolic material, non-human efficacy, and the difference between felt force and authorized legitimacy can be observed. Its methodological contribution is the pressure that later becomes a refusal to treat self-consistency or apparent efficacy as verification.

HEXA / Hexadecimal Mystic is a distributed writing laboratory. Its current public surface is Hexadecimal Mystic on Substack; its earlier writing substrate includes le malek exotérique. The WordPress archive is included as intellectual and stylistic substrate: symbolic, ritual, Hermetic, urban, and recursive writing. Its blog-post dates do not establish the date of HEXA or the origin date of PHAROS.

Together, these laboratories make the progression from raw material to rendering, comparative reading, drift detection, and governance control possible. Neither is an agent, a skill, nor proof that every associated creative work was written as AI-governance research.

Provenance ruleA public surface can document a body of practice without fixing the origin date of the laboratory it belongs to. Surface dates, archive dates, and method-formation dates remain separate.

vault cross-check · formation details

The vault makes the formation more granular

September 2024 – April 2026documented

PHAROS is already a five-lane program before the software crystallizes

The strongest archive checkpoint is not a single software artifact. It is the September 2024 research retrospective, where the future method is visible as several parallel lines of work.

The September 2024 checkpoint shows five active lanes forming together: PHAROS methodology, institutional AI and power, cultural studies, neo-Pagan and queer theory, and clinical AI or health governance. That matters because the method does not emerge from one disciplinary origin or one product build. It becomes legible at the intersection of ritual studies, governance, cultural analysis, disability and fluency work, and clinical AI accountability.

The date sequence also needs to stay separated. The vault distinguishes late-2024 to early-2025 conception, an initial operational articulation around 21 March 2025, and the formal invention disclosure filed on 1 April 2026. Those are three different kinds of evidence: conceptual formation, working articulation, and legal/IP filing.

Within the laboratory phase, the distributed traces around Wheels of Will, HEXA, SAKURA, Sealed Card, and Charging Objects supply the bridge vocabulary: glitch, seam, charging, legibility, and legitimacy. Their role is to make recursive rewriting, drift, and authority inflation observable before those risks become software controls.

BoundaryThese vault additions strengthen the formation account, but they do not turn every creative surface into origin proof. They document substrate, pressure, vocabulary, and method formation; origin, archive, laboratory, and product dates remain separate.
1 March – 29 April 2026documented

The architecture changes from operator model to governed stack

The ten-week compression is not only a rush of software specification. It also contains a governance-architecture transition that explains how the method stops depending on one blended voice.

On 1 March 2026, the Eight Operators model becomes inactive and the Three-Agent Stack becomes operational. The old operator functions do not disappear; they are redistributed into HEPHAISTOS, Queen Keyport, Hermes, and Argus. On 23 April 2026, Linear Authority is superseded by a Co-Equal Authority model. On 29 April 2026, the Second Self routing layer is added, making public voice, execution, orchestration, memory, and governance roles easier to keep separate.

compression · February–April 2026

Sixteen years of ideas turn into ten weeks of software

17 February – 27 April 2026documented

The rules become something a machine can actually enforce

Of everything in this history, this window carries the most weight: about ten weeks in which analysis turns into something a piece of software can actually be held to.

On 17 February 2026, the first written specification of how the system should "speak" — its voice, in effect — is produced. One day later, on 18 February, that specification is instantiated as a working AI assistant: the voice is written down, then brought to life the next day. Over the following weeks, two separate families of rules — one governing tone and prose, the other governing safety and process — are merged, on 28 March, into a single deployable specification: seven layers of rules fused into one. The resulting set of eight named roles is then translated, on 27 April, into the three-part structure the system still runs on today — a builder role, an authority role, and a messenger role, each carried over under a new name without changing what the role actually does. Two supporting roles are added shortly after, on 8 May.

Sixteen years of scholarly development compress into roughly ten weeks of software specification. None of the technical work in this window invents anything genuinely new — it translates commitments already fixed, in academic prose, over a decade and a half. A long, slow intellectual formation and a short, fast technical encoding sit directly against each other here.

2026

Finding out what breaks

2026documented

Once rules can be enforced, the next step is finding where they fail

An enforceable method is only as good as its account of its own failure modes — the method turning its own controls back on itself.

Once governance rules could actually be enforced by software, the necessary next step was identifying what breaks when they are: where an enforceable rule can still be gamed, ignored, or misapplied. This period builds in the checks — gates, markers, handoffs, and refusal conditions — that catch those failures before they cause harm. None of the underlying commitments here are new; what's new is making them hold up under pressure.

2026

The method states its own limit

2026documented

Verification Without Trust, and The Pharos Frame

A method that can enforce its own rules eventually has to answer an uncomfortable question about itself: can it be trusted when the evidence for its own correctness comes from the system itself?

Two connected pieces of work, Verification Without Trust and The Pharos Frame, take this on directly. Their answer is a limit, not a reassurance: self-generated evidence is only admissible to the extent that it leaves behind something that can be checked independently — logs, timestamps, citations, something outside the system's own say-so. This is presented as the method's honest limit rather than proof of its own reliability, and it deliberately reopens older questions about authority and legitimacy rather than closing them for good.

product surfaces · no GPT roster

The method becomes a product family

April – July 2026documented

The non-GPT products split into governance tools, intake surfaces, and adjacent apps

This is the commercial and operational layer that grows around PHAROS. It should be counted as productization, not as the origin of the method itself.

The central governance stack is PHAROS-AI.CA, HELIX, COMPASSai, AurorA, and the Hermes Dashboard. PHAROS-AI.CA is the public webservice and commercial front door. HELIX productizes PHAROS as a guided or self-serve four-phase governance stress-test runner. COMPASSai is the governance engine. AurorA is the client-facing intake and evidence-legibility module that feeds COMPASSai. Hermes Dashboard is the operator-facing activity and routing surface.

RAGE, the Recursive Artifact Governance Engine, is a graph-native proposal for storing prompts, outputs, critiques, revisions, decisions, and evidence as traceable artifacts, then scoring recursive risks such as drift, authority amplification, and provenance loss. It belongs in the product lineage as design intent and market positioning, not as a deployed runtime unless implementation evidence is later attached.

Other non-GPT product surfaces include ComplyScan, a bilingual Law 25 / AI Act compliance SaaS concept; the Obsidian Agent Vault / Second Brain product, where EMERAULD functions as proof of concept; clearday, a bilingual mobile app for perimenopause tracking and AI-assisted education; Montréal+, a civic-assistant mobile app with unresolved fork and billing alignment issues; nexusos, a Base44 internal strategy and operations command center; and corpus-5point, a FastAPI/Next.js research-intelligence platform with rights-aware ingestion.

7 May – 16 May 2026, then ongoingdocumented

The Fisher King era turns product sprawl into recovery work

By May 2026, the problem is no longer only invention or launch. It is project recovery: deciding which products are alive, which are stalled, which should be healed, and which should be held or archived.

The Percephal Diagnostic Protocol, created in the vault on 7 May 2026, translates the Fisher King problem into a project-recovery method. A stalled project is treated as a wounded but still load-bearing system. The healing act is not adding more material; it is asking the project-state question that names the wound, the current artifact, the contradiction to preserve, the blocker, and the next synthesis move.

The Fisher King Hub, created on 16 May 2026, organizes the recovery map by wound severity rather than by domain. Tier 1 contains the load-bearing wounds: PHAROS, HELIX, Second Brain, Second Self, and Papers. Tier 2 contains supporting systems including LOTUS, GAIA, Dr. Sort, Echo, Scripto/Scriptorium, COMPASSai, and AurorA. Tier 3 contains downstream or secondary wounds such as Glitching the Sacred, Stuttering Machines, and Magie sanguine.

This era matters to the formation of the method because it makes a second kind of governance visible: not only how to judge AI outputs, but how to prevent a growing portfolio of AI products, papers, archives, and agents from becoming unreadable to its own operator.

Recovery ruleFisher King status is not a failure label. It is a governance state: the project still matters, but its next move has to be named before more accumulation can count as progress.

2026 — present

Where things stand, and a return to where they started

August 2026documented

The method now runs as software as well as scholarship

The method stops living only in papers and starts running as software: skills and agents that can be called across different platforms without duplicating the underlying rules each time.

As of August 2026, the method exists both as an ongoing body of academic writing and as running software. Not every product built on top of it, and not every earlier piece of cultural analysis, belongs to the same lineage just because it now sits under the same name — the connection has to be earned by what a given piece of work actually does, not assumed from proximity.

29 July 2026, looking back to 2010documented

A new essay returns to the same two novels examined sixteen years earlier

This is the one place in the whole history where the timeline doesn't move forward — it moves back, deliberately, and that return is itself worth noting.

In the same period, a short essay — Pourquoi rêver encore? Lecture allégorique et métaphysique de l'œuvre initiale d'Yvon Rivard, roughly 5,000 words — returns to the same two novels examined in the original 2010 thesis, revisiting the same triad of soul, double, and shadow. This time it's read through newer critical tools — Donna Haraway's work on situated knowledge, and Victor Turner's work on liminality — rather than abandoning the earlier framework. The same structural commitment that opened this history in 2010 is still the one being tested, sixteen years later, in both the software and the scholarship.

The essay's publication status is unsettled: separate records describe it as published, as under review, and as still in draft. The record that carries the latest date says it is not finished.
